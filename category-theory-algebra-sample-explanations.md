## 1. Time as a category in computation

The fundamental object is the monoid

$$
(\mathbb N,+,0)
$$

of computation steps.

Every monoid is a one-object category, so define a category (T):

* one object:

$$
*
$$

* morphisms:

$$
\text{Hom}(*,*) = \mathbb N
$$

* composition:

$$
m \circ n = m+n
$$

* identity morphism:

$$
id_* = 0
$$

Diagrammatically:

```text
* --1--> *
* --2--> *
* --3--> *
```

but since there is only one object, these arrows are all endomorphisms.

Composition means:

```text
wait 3 steps then 5 steps
=
wait 8 steps
```

---

## 2. Computation is a functor from time

Suppose program states form a set (X).

A deterministic program transition is

$$
F:X\to X
$$

Then define:

$$
F^n:X\to X
$$

where

$$
F^0=id
$$

$$
F^1=F
$$

$$
F^2=F\circ F
$$

etc.

Categorically:

$$
\mathcal T \to \mathbf{Set}
$$

mapping:

* the single object to (X)
* time morphism (n) to (F^n)

Thus a program is literally:

> a representation of the time monoid.

---

## 3. Orbit of a program state

For initial state

$$
x_0
$$

orbit is

$$
\mathcal O(x_0)
\\=======\\\\=======\\=

{x_0,F(x_0),F^2(x_0),...}
$$

This is simply:

* reachable states
* search space explored by BFS/DFS
* DP state graph
* simulation trajectory

# Example 1: Floyd cycle detection

Problem:

```text
x_{n+1}=f(x_n)
```

Example:

$$
f(x)=(x^2+1)\mod 255
$$

Orbit:

$$
x_0
\to x_1
\to x_2
\to x_3
\to ...
$$

Finite state space implies eventually:

```text
tail -> cycle
```

Formally:

$$
\mathcal O(x)
\\=======\\======

\text{transient part}
\sqcup
\text{periodic orbit}
$$

The cycle itself is a group action:

$$
\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z
$$

acting by rotation.

---

Invariant:

```text
visited states set
```

or

```text
distance from first visit modulo cycle length
```

---

Stabilizer:

For state (x),

$$
Stab(x)
\\=======\\

{n:F^n(x)=x}
$$

If cycle length is (k),

$$
Stab(x)=k\mathbb N
$$

This is a submonoid.

---

# Example 2: Sliding window

Problem:

Find longest subarray with sum ≤ K.

State:

$$
(l,r,s)
$$

Transition monoid generated by:

```text
expand right
shrink left
```

Orbit:

```text
(0,0,0)
→
(0,1,a_0)
→
(0,2,a_0+a_1)
→
...
```

Invariant:

$$
s=\sum_{i=l}^{r-1}a_i
$$

The entire algorithm works because transition maps preserve:

$$
s
=

\text{window sum}
$$

This is not a stabilizer.

It is an invariant function

$$
I:X\to Y
$$

such that

$$
I(F(x))
\\=======\\

T(I(x))
$$

or often

$$
I(F(x))
\\=======\\

I(x)
$$

# Example 3: Union Find

Operation monoid:

$$
M=\langle union,find\rangle
$$

State space:

all forest configurations.

Orbit:

all forests reachable from initial singleton partition.

Invariant:

connected components partition.

Union preserves:

$$
x\sim y
$$

being an equivalence relation.

---

Stabilizer:

after component becomes fully merged:

```text
union(a,b)
```

acts trivially.

Thus some operations enter stabilizer of current state.

# Example 4: Prefix sums

Transition:

$$
S_{i+1}=S_i+a_i
$$

Orbit:

$$
0,S_1,S_2,...,S_n
$$

Invariant discovered:

$$
sum(l,r)
\\=======\\=

S_r-S_l
$$

This invariant converts:

```text
range query
```

into:

```text
difference in orbit positions
```

# Example 5: Binary search

State:

$$
(l,r)
$$

Transition:

$$
(l,r)
\to
(l,m)
$$

or

$$
(l,r)
\to
(m+1,r)
$$

Orbit:

nested intervals.

Invariant:

target lies in interval.

$$
target\in$$l,r$$
$$

This invariant is preserved under every morphism.

# Example 6: Dijkstra

State:

$$
(dist,Q)
$$

Time morphism:

extract minimum and relax edges.

Orbit:

sequence of frontier states.

Fundamental invariant:

$$
dist$$v$$
$$

is shortest discovered path length.

When vertex leaves heap:

$$
dist$$v$$
\\=======\\

d^*(v)
$$

becomes fixed forever.

This is a stabilizer-like phenomenon:

future time morphisms act trivially on that coordinate.

# Example 7: Two pointers on sorted array

Invariant:

$$
i<j
$$

and

$$
A_i+A_j
$$

moves monotonically.

Orbit:

path through lattice

$$
(i,j)
\in
$$0,n$$^2
$$

The algorithm succeeds because orbit is monotone and never revisits states.

# Are invariants stabilizers?

Not generally.

## Stabilizer

Subset of operations fixing a state:

$$
Stab(x)
\\=======\\

{m:m\cdot x=x}
$$

---

## Invariant

Function

$$
I:X\to Y
$$

constant under action:

$$
I(m\cdot x)=I(x)
$$

for all (m).

---

Example:

Parity of permutation under legal moves in the 15-puzzle:

$$
I(\sigma)
\\=======\\==

\text{parity}(\sigma)
$$

Moves do not fix the state.

Moves preserve parity.

Thus parity is an invariant but not a stabilizer.

## Competitive programming meta-template

Almost every problem can be written as:

### State space

$$
X
$$

---

### Time monoid

$$
M=(\mathbb N,+)
$$

or operation monoid generated by moves.

---

### Action

$$
M\curvearrowright X
$$

---

### Orbit

reachable states.

---

### Invariant

quantity preserved by action.

---

### Monotone quantity

quantity that strictly increases or decreases.

---

### Fixed points

states satisfying

$$
F(x)=x
$$

---

### Stabilizer

operations that no longer change state.

---

### Quotient by invariant

compress equivalent states into one representative.

Examples:

* DSU
* memoization
* DP state compression
* modulo arithmetic
* canonical forms

---

This viewpoint is remarkably close to how experienced competitive programmers think:

> Define the state category, understand the action of time/operations on that state, identify invariants and monotone quantities, and exploit them to reduce the reachable orbit of the computation.
