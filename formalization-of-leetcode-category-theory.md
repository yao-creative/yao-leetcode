A clean way to model what you’re doing is to separate **intent → search space → evaluation → construction**, then lift it into algebra.

---

## 1. Intent layer (problem specification)

A LeetCode problem is essentially a **constraint satisfaction + optimization problem over programs**:

You are given:

* Input space: ( X )
* Output space: ( Y )
* Validity predicate (constraints):
  $$
  C : X \times Y \to {\text{true}, \text{false}}
  $$
* Objective (implicit or explicit):
  $$
  \text{optimize } f(x, y) \text{ (time, space, or simplicity)}
  $$

So the *spec* is:

$$
\forall x \in X,; \text{find } y \in Y \text{ such that } C(x,y)=\text{true}
$$

or sometimes:

$$
y^* = \arg\min_{y \in Y} f(x,y) ;; \text{s.t. } C(x,y)
$$

---

## 2. Your real search space = program space (monoidal structure)

Instead of directly searching (Y), you are implicitly searching over **programs**:

* Let ( $ \mathcal{P} $ ) be programs that map ( $X \to Y$)
* Execution is an evaluation morphism:
  $$
  \llbracket \cdot \rrbracket : \mathcal{P} \to (X \to Y)
  $$

Now the key structure:

### Composition = monoid

Programs compose:

* Sequential composition: ( $p_1 \circ p_2$ )
* Identity program: ( $\text{id}$ )

So:

$$
(\mathcal{P}, \circ, \text{id})
$$

is a **monoid (often enriched with types / effects)**.

---

## 3. What you actually do: constrained program synthesis

You are solving:

$$
p^* = \arg\min_{p \in \mathcal{P}} ; \text{Cost}(p)
\quad \text{s.t. } \forall x,; C(x, p(x))
$$

But you *never search the full monoid*. Instead:

### You restrict to an algorithmic submonoid:

You implicitly choose a sub-algebra:

$$
\mathcal{A} \subset \mathcal{P}
$$

generated by:

* recursion schemes
* traversal patterns (DFS/BFS/DP folds)
* data structure operations

So your real task is:

> pick the correct **algebraic basis** (recursion scheme) and instantiate it.

---

## 4. Preorder / ordering intuition (what you noticed)

When you say:

> “find a preorder and build traversal order”

That corresponds to:

### You are constructing a morphism from a poset

You define:

* a state space (S)
* a preorder ( $\preceq$ ) on states
* a traversal = monotone map:

$$
f : (S, \preceq) \to (Y, \le)
$$

So algorithms are often:

> monotone structure-preserving maps over an ordered state space

---

## 5. Core abstraction: recursion schemes = your “algorithm templates”

Most LeetCode solutions are instantiations of:

### (A) Catamorphism (fold)

$$
\text{fold} : F(A) \to A
$$

Used in:

* DP
* tree aggregation
* interval merging

---

### (B) Anamorphism (unfold)

$$
A \to F(A)
$$

Used in:

* BFS generation
* state expansion
* backtracking tree construction

---

### (C) Hylomorphism (unfold + fold)

$$
A \to F(A) \to B
$$

Used in:

* “generate then compress”
* search + evaluate pipelines

---

So your “pick correct algorithm” step is actually:

> choosing the correct recursion scheme over the problem functor (F)

---

## 6. The “LeetCode decision process” as a categorical pipeline

You are doing:

### Step 1: interpret problem as functor

$$
F : \text{State} \to \text{StateSpace}
$$

### Step 2: choose algebra

$$
\alpha : F(A) \to A
$$

### Step 3: choose traversal order (strategy)

This is the key part you mentioned:

* DFS = depth-first unfolding strategy
* BFS = breadth-first colimit construction
* DP = memoized catamorphism over DAG

So traversal is:

$$
\text{strategy} : \text{coalgebra structure}
$$

---

## 7. Final compact “closed form”

A very compact formalization of what you're doing:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Solve LeetCode} =
\arg\min_{\alpha \in \text{Alg}(F)} ;
\text{Cost}(\text{hylo}(\alpha))
}
$$

where:

* (F) = problem structure (tree, graph, sequence, DP state graph)
* ( $ \text{Alg}(F)$ ) = valid recursion schemes
* ( $ \text{hylo} $ ) = unfold + fold execution
* Cost = time/space complexity
